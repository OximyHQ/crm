# AI-native CRM and prospecting landscape

**Research date:** 2026-08-18
**Audience:** Oximy founders and the three-person sales team
**Decision:** Improve Oximy CRM before replacing it

## Purpose

This report compares modern CRM and prospecting products against Oximy's current sales workflow.

The research covers record capture, enrichment, prioritization, relationship intelligence, outreach, agents, integrations, MCP, and pricing.

The report uses current public sources.

The report does not test paid product accounts.

## Evidence labels

- **Verified** means an official product, documentation, pricing, or help page supports the statement.
- **Customer evidence** means a named customer story or independent review supports the statement.
- **Inference** means the report applies a verified pattern to Oximy's workflow.
- **Unknown** means public evidence does not answer the question.

Vendor outcome claims are not independent evidence.

They show product positioning and possible results.

They do not predict Oximy's results.

## Research method

Parallel Search found current product pages, documentation, pricing, reviews, and changelogs.

Normal web search verified important details against official sources.

The current Oximy architecture comes from the repository architecture reference.

The comparison emphasizes a ten-person company with three GTM users.

## Executive conclusions

1. Oximy needs one operating loop, not another isolated prospect database.
2. Automatic capture and enrichment must start before a seller creates a record.
3. The system must show why each account ranks highly.
4. One score cannot represent product fit, timing, relationship quality, and contact fit.
5. The daily work queue matters more than a complex dashboard.
6. Relationship evidence should include team interactions, job tenure, role history, and warm paths.
7. Outreach should use product-specific research, person-specific value, and explicit approval controls.
8. MCP should make CRM data usable from ChatGPT, Claude, and future Cardinal agents.
9. CRM records need outcome feedback from messages, replies, meetings, opportunities, and losses.
10. Oximy should preserve its internal agent architecture and add focused GTM skills.

The strongest small-team pattern combines Clarify, folk, Apollo, and Day AI concepts.

Clarify shows a compact autonomous workflow inside one CRM.

folk shows fast LinkedIn capture and relationship follow-up.

Apollo shows searchable people data, enrichment, scoring, and sequencing.

Day AI shows shared customer memory and skills across every conversation.

Attio shows a strong programmable data platform and external agent surface.

Affinity shows the best relationship graph pattern.

Common Room shows the mature buyer-signal architecture.

Clay shows the strongest configurable enrichment and research pipeline.

Salesforce shows enterprise agent depth.

Salesforce does not fit Oximy's present company size or operating model.

## Oximy's current job

The team starts with a manually created company list.

The list assigns Relay, Sidekick, or Visibility fit through human judgment.

A seller creates a company record manually.

The seller also finds the company domain manually.

CRM enrichment often returns no useful result.

The seller then searches Apollo for relevant senior people.

The seller uses titles and management levels as search filters.

The seller copies selected contacts into CRM.

The seller enriches email, phone, and LinkedIn data.

The seller reads LinkedIn career history.

Long tenure suggests a company-value pitch.

Frequent movement suggests a personal-value pitch.

The seller chooses outreach targets through judgment.

This process splits discovery, data entry, enrichment, research, selection, and outreach across many surfaces.

The process also discards most decision evidence.

The next seller cannot inspect why another seller selected or rejected an account.

## Recurring market patterns

### 1. Passive capture replaces manual CRM hygiene

Day AI, Clarify, Affinity, Attio, folk, and HubSpot start from communications.

They create context from email, calendar, meetings, calls, and messages.

Oximy already ingests many of these sources.

The missing step is turning them into seller-ready records and recommendations.

### 2. Enrichment uses providers, waterfalls, and provenance

Apollo, Clay, Common Room, HubSpot, folk, and Affinity use multiple data sources.

One provider failure does not end the workflow.

Successful systems retain partial results.

They also expose source, age, confidence, or provider details.

### 3. Prioritization separates fit from timing

Apollo, Common Room, Unify, HubSpot, and Cardinal combine static and changing data.

Static signals include industry, size, geography, technology, and title.

Changing signals include hiring, job moves, funding, web visits, replies, and meetings.

A tier represents fit.

A queue position represents current action value.

These values should remain separate.

### 4. Relationship context changes outreach

Affinity scores team relationship strength.

folk summarizes interaction history and follow-up needs.

Attio tracks strongest connections.

Day AI reasons across the complete customer story.

Oximy's tenure heuristic belongs inside a broader relationship profile.

The profile should explain the suggested pitch.

### 5. AI works through bounded jobs

Clarify uses sales agents and custom agents.

Day AI uses roles, skills, schedules, and events.

HubSpot uses prospecting plays and guardrails.

Twenty exposes custom skills through its app model.

One generic chat cannot replace these bounded jobs.

### 6. Review precedes autonomy

HubSpot supports reviewed drafts before fully autonomous email.

folk shows draft suggestions before sending.

Agentforce uses explicit handoff and permissions.

Oximy should use preview, approval, and audit stages.

### 7. MCP becomes a standard CRM surface

Attio, folk, Twenty, Apollo, Affinity, and Day AI publish MCP capabilities.

MCP lets sellers use CRM data from their preferred assistant.

The CRM remains the policy and audit boundary.

### 8. Pricing follows work, credits, or agents

Clarify charges for AI work instead of seats.

Day AI charges for deployed agents.

HubSpot charges for recommended outreach outcomes.

Apollo, Clay, Unify, and Attio use credits.

Oximy needs cost records per enrichment, research run, and outreach candidate.

## Direct implications for Oximy

### Product principle

The CRM should become a system of action with a durable memory.

The browser UI remains useful for review, configuration, exceptions, and analytics.

ChatGPT, Claude, Slack, and Cardinal can become daily interaction surfaces.

All surfaces should use the same permissioned CRM tools.

### Company intake

A seller should paste any company name, URL, LinkedIn URL, or text fragment.

The system should resolve the canonical company and domain.

The system should show alternative matches before creating ambiguous records.

The system should deduplicate against domain, LinkedIn identifier, and known aliases.

It should immediately start company enrichment and product-fit research.

### Contact discovery

Each company should offer a `Find buying committee` action.

The action should use Apollo now.

Future Cardinal and other providers can join the same boundary.

The result should group people by role.

Suggested groups include economic buyer, technical buyer, security reviewer, champion, and influencer.

Each person needs title evidence and current-employment verification.

### Enrichment waterfall

Enrichment must operate per field.

Email, phone, LinkedIn, title, company, and location need independent states.

Each state needs `found`, `not_found`, `stale`, `conflict`, or `blocked`.

Each value needs provider, timestamp, evidence, confidence, and cost.

The UI should show partial success.

The agent should retry only eligible fields.

### Explainable product fit

Each company needs separate Relay, Sidekick, and Visibility fit results.

Each result needs a score, tier, confidence, supporting evidence, and counter-evidence.

The score should never hide exclusions.

Initial scoring should use an explicit rule set.

The agent can add researched evidence.

Humans must see the rule contribution.

### Person and pitch intelligence

The person profile should summarize career tenure and movement.

The summary should distinguish stable operators, recent joiners, founders, and frequent movers.

The system should propose a pitch angle.

Company-value pitches should focus on organizational outcomes.

Personal-value pitches should focus on mandate, credibility, speed, and career impact.

The system should cite the evidence behind each recommendation.

### Daily action queue

The CRM home should answer one question.

It should show who deserves attention today and explain why.

Queue ranking should combine product fit, person fit, timing, relationship, and engagement.

Sellers should record `useful`, `wrong account`, `wrong person`, `bad timing`, or `bad data`.

These decisions create training and evaluation data.

### Outreach workflow

The system should draft messages for email and LinkedIn.

It should not automate LinkedIn actions without approved integration and policy review.

Each draft should name its research evidence and pitch hypothesis.

The seller should approve, edit, skip, or schedule the action.

The CRM should store the final message and the generated draft.

Reply classification should stop unsuitable sequences automatically.

Meeting booking should create a preparation task automatically.

### Skills for the main CRM agent

Oximy's agent already supports tools, skills, schedules, hooks, and subagents.

The missing work is a focused GTM skill library.

| Skill | Trigger | Main output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `resolve-company` | New company input | Canonical company, domain, aliases, and confidence |
| `find-buying-committee` | Company action | Relevant people grouped by buying role |
| `enrich-prospect` | New or incomplete record | Field-level values with provenance |
| `score-product-fit` | New evidence or ICP change | Three product-fit scorecards |
| `analyze-career-pattern` | New person | Tenure pattern and pitch implication |
| `prepare-outreach` | Seller queue action | Evidence-based email and LinkedIn drafts |
| `monitor-signals` | Schedule or webhook | Timing changes and queue updates |
| `prepare-meeting` | Calendar event | Account brief, people brief, risks, and questions |
| `record-outcome` | Send, reply, meeting, or loss | Structured outcome and model feedback |
| `recalibrate-icp` | Weekly or monthly review | Segment performance and proposed rule changes |

Each skill needs test fixtures and evaluation criteria.

Each skill needs allowed tools and data scopes.

Each skill must expose its evidence and uncertainty.

### MCP as the interaction layer

The CRM already exposes remote MCP.

The next MCP version should support complete prospecting workflows.

Required operations include company resolution, provider search, enrichment, scoring, queue retrieval, and outreach drafts.

Write tools need idempotency and approval boundaries.

External agents should never receive provider secrets.

The CRM should log every external action with user, client, scope, input, and result.

This approach lets sellers work inside ChatGPT or Claude.

CRM remains available for browsing, correction, and audit.

### GTM consolidation

`gtm.oximy.com` should not remain a separate destination.

Its useful search, ICP, list, and scoring functions should move behind CRM services.

The migration should preserve saved lists, score explanations, and search evidence.

The ClickHouse prospect dataset can remain a specialized backend.

CRM should own the durable prospect, activity, and outcome records.

## Recommended build order

### Phase 0: Measure the baseline

Run this phase for two weeks.

Track company entry time, enrichment success, contact entry time, research time, and first-touch time.

Track positive replies, qualified meetings, opportunities, and disqualification reasons.

Record the denominator for every stage.

Do not use meeting counts without prospect and outreach counts.

### Phase 1: Remove manual transfer

Add universal company intake.

Add canonical domain resolution and deduplication.

Embed Apollo company and people search.

Add bulk import from the Fast Start sheet.

Add field-level enrichment states and provenance.

This phase should remove the largest current time loss.

### Phase 2: Make selection explainable

Create separate product-fit scorecards.

Create buying-role classification.

Create career-pattern summaries and pitch angles.

Add a ranked daily action queue.

Collect seller feedback on every recommendation.

### Phase 3: Connect research to outreach

Add reusable outreach skills per Oximy product.

Add reviewed email and LinkedIn drafts.

Add follow-up reminders and stopping rules.

Add meeting preparation and post-meeting updates.

### Phase 4: Learn from outcomes

Link scores to sends, replies, meetings, opportunities, wins, and losses.

Measure results by segment, product, persona, channel, and pitch angle.

Run monthly ICP recalibration with human approval.

Promote rules only after retrospective evaluation.

### Phase 5: Consolidate surfaces

Move GTM search and scoring into CRM services.

Expand remote MCP for complete seller workflows.

Connect Cardinal MCP when it becomes available.

Keep the CRM UI for inspection, configuration, reporting, and exceptions.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Core CRM | Build on current CRM | Oximy already owns records, communications, agents, and MCP |
| People database | Integrate Apollo | The team already uses it and trusts its workflow |
| Precision outbound | Pilot Cardinal | The team has access and the product fits founder-led sales |
| Enrichment orchestration | Build provider boundary | Oximy needs provenance, fallback, and durable state |
| Research and scoring | Build as agent skills | Product knowledge and evidence are Oximy-specific |
| Email sequencing | Build minimal or integrate | Start with review-first workflow and measured volume |
| LinkedIn capture | Build extension or approved connector | Manual entry is costly and current context is valuable |
| LinkedIn automation | Defer | Policy, reliability, and account safety require review |
| Relationship graph | Build from captured activity | CRM already stores email, calendar, call, and message evidence |
| Buyer intent | Integrate later | Clean identity and outcome data must come first |
| External assistant access | Expand MCP | The existing architecture already supports this direction |

## Pilot plan for three sellers

Run one controlled pilot for four weeks.

Use 150 new accounts across Relay, Sidekick, and Visibility hypotheses.

Assign equal account groups across the present workflow and improved workflow.

Keep seller skill and source mix balanced.

Measure these outcomes:

- Minutes from account discovery to complete CRM record.
- Percentage of companies with a verified canonical domain.
- Percentage of selected people with verified current employment.
- Percentage of contacts with usable email, phone, and LinkedIn data.
- Seller agreement with each product-fit score.
- Seller agreement with each pitch angle.
- Time from record creation to first approved touch.
- Positive reply rate per delivered message.
- Qualified meeting rate per contacted account.
- Opportunity rate per qualified meeting.
- Wrong-account, wrong-person, bad-timing, and bad-data rates.
- Cost per complete record and qualified meeting.

The primary metric should be qualified meetings per contacted account.

The guardrail metric should be seller time per qualified meeting.

The system should also protect sender reputation and LinkedIn accounts.

## Issues

1. BROKEN — Current enrichment often returns no useful result. Sellers repeat the work in Apollo.
   Fix: build field-level provider waterfalls with partial results and evidence.
2. BROKEN — Sellers enter companies, domains, and contacts manually. Prospect setup consumes selling time.
   Fix: add universal intake, deduplication, browser capture, and embedded provider search.
3. RISK — Gut-feel scores lack durable evidence. The team cannot improve selection rules reliably.
   Fix: store product-specific scorecards, evidence, counter-evidence, and seller feedback.
4. RISK — A separate GTM database splits selection evidence from sales outcomes. ICP learning remains incomplete.
   Fix: make CRM the durable prospect and outcome store.
5. RISK — Automated LinkedIn actions can violate platform rules or damage seller accounts.
   Fix: defer automation until legal, policy, and reliability reviews finish.
6. UNKNOWN — Public Cardinal pages do not show pricing, MCP actions, or integration limits.
   Fix: test the product account and inspect MCP documentation after access arrives.
7. UNKNOWN — Public feature pages do not prove enrichment accuracy for Oximy's target accounts.
   Fix: run the controlled pilot with verified field-level results.
8. UNKNOWN — Repository research does not measure the team's present qualified-meeting baseline.
   Fix: instrument the baseline before product changes.
